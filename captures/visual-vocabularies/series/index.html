Fixed grammar / Carbon & citrus

Carbon & citrus

Interrupted blue contours. One solid orange. Room for the page to breathe.

Could serve: Text-rich editorial pages, gentle spot illustrations and clear adult-learning scenes.

Three neighbours

The family is immediately legible in blue and orange, and the listening gesture reads. Stair edges and shoelaces reclaim much of the detail the prompt asked to omit.

Exact prompt
Use case: illustration-story. Create one standalone square image for a comparative visual-language study. This is an original new composition, not an imitation of a particular artist. Show the flat illustration filling the square, not a photographed print or mockup. No lettering, numerals, labels, signatures, watermarks or decorative frame. Preserve the specified number and physical relationship of the subjects. Contemporary ordinary life, unidealised people if any, no uplifting symbols or generic charity-ad poses.
SCENE: Three adult neighbours having an ordinary conversation on a broad front step. An older woman sits on the upper step at left, a heavy-set middle-aged man leans against a plain doorframe at right, and a young adult with a shaved head sits sideways one step lower. One speaks with a small open-hand gesture, the other two listen. They wear ordinary contemporary clothes. A small folded shopping bag rests on the lowest step. No tea cups, flowers, street scenery or symbolic decorations. Keep all three people recognisable as separate bodies and make the gestures quiet, not a posed group portrait. The designated accent is the folded bag.
FIXED STYLE GRAMMAR: A sparse editorial drawing transferred through exhausted blue carbon paper onto clean bright white paper. Broken ultramarine-blue lines, pressure gaps, occasional faint double tracks and incomplete contours describe the scene. Large areas remain entirely untouched. Do not enclose every shape or shade skin, fabric or objects. Faces, if present, use a few unequal strokes without rendered eyes, eyelashes, cheeks or hair strands. Hands are readable through their meeting edges, not detailed fingers. Every mark is a line except ONE flat, opaque vermilion-orange accent on the object specified in the scene. That accent is the only solid colour. No blue filled silhouettes, gray shadows, wash, pencil shading, cartoon prettiness, picturesque setting, paper stains or all-over texture. The incomplete drawing and solid accent are the grammar; no orange fruit is required.

Fixed grammar / Stencil + red pencil

Stencil + red pencil

A broad green template, a small red observation, one orange accent.

Could serve: Contemporary editorial series, posters and a coordinated family of service or learning images.

Three neighbours

The three bodies separate clearly at small size. The red hands and profiles have a different role from the green clothing. Most white interruptions read as folds rather than functional stencil bridges.

FIXED STYLE GRAMMAR: An original hybrid editorial illustration on clean bright white. Broad flat dark petrol-green shapes are cut with reusable industrial stencils. Preserve clean narrow white support bridges that interrupt enclosed stencil forms. Use these rigid masses for clothing, major objects or supporting structures. A thin fragile dry RED COLOUR PENCIL line supplies small specific observations: profiles, the meeting of hands, a bent leaf or an object's rim. Red line has its own job; it does not trace every green silhouette. Keep faces unmodelled and hair a simple stencil shape. The single designated scene accent is one opaque burnt-orange area. Clean white gaps and a strong asymmetrical composition do the rest. No gray modelling, realistic faces, soft gradients, red cheek hatching, all-over grain, spray paint, splashes, military lettering, decorative faux misregistration or nostalgia. The large green template and the small red observation must remain visibly different tools.

Fixed grammar / White-line engraving

White-line engraving

A black field. White incisions. The spacing of cuts makes the light.

Could serve: Literary covers, selected chapter images and stories that benefit from density and gravity.

Three neighbours

The medium holds, but nearly every surface becomes worked. The ordinary conversation acquires a solemn weight. The dark door supplies one of the few real pauses in the marks.

FIXED STYLE GRAMMAR: A one-colour black relief impression interpreted as fine white-line engraving cut with a burin into end-grain boxwood. Build from solid BLACK; light is made by fine WHITE incisions. Vary spacing, direction and the length of cuts according to the form. Short wedge cuts and uncut dark areas must alternate with restrained groups of curving fine furrows. Keep broad dark masses and decisive uncut silences; do not coat every surface in perfectly uniform contour bands. Faces and hands are observed but not glamorous. A mundane action can have the gravity of an emblem. Edges and form come from white cuts, not black pencil contours over white. The designated coloured accent in the subject must translate into a particularly clear white shape, because this entire image is MONOCHROME. No orange ink, grey wash, photographic grayscale, fine-line filter pasted over a photo, visible woodgrain across the artwork, antique-paper stains, sepia, ornamental borders or nostalgic costume.

A separate editing experiment

Remove the wear.
Keep the mark.

I asked the Eraser to remove the chipped-paint costume from the stencil still life. That edit also smoothed away much of the edge character. A second edit restored some blunt changes of direction and made the red observations more partial. The red still behaves more like an outline than the dry pencil I wanted.

The nine-image comparison above keeps the untouched first passes. These revisions show the cost of one art-direction decision.

Both exact edit prompts and image roles

First edit

Edit the supplied stencil + red-pencil still life. Preserve the square crop, kettle at left, pear at right, folded striped cloth below, petrol-green / red-pencil / burnt-orange / clean-white palette, and every object's position and scale. Remove the cosmetic wear from the image. Eliminate the scattered white chips, scraped patches, jagged flaked edges and random scratches from the kettle body, handle and pear. Make the large petrol-green areas smooth and opaque. Keep the kettle's one broad dent in its overall contour; do not add surface marks to explain it. Express the reusable stencil construction using a few clean straight white support bridges in the handle ring and where a closed interior needs support. These bridges are deliberate unpainted cuts, not chipped enamel. Keep the red pencil only at the kettle rim, spout lip and pear stem; remove incidental red tracing around most of the pear's perimeter. Keep the two green cloth stripes and its one solid orange folded edge, with plain white fabric between. No new objects, shadows, lettering or decorative texture. The intent is a crisp reusable shape grammar with small specific red observations, without a distressed-print costume. The supplied image is the edit target.

Second edit

Image 1: clean edit target. Image 2: first-pass material reference.

Image 1 is the edit target: the clean green stencil still life. Image 2 is a material-edge reference only: the first, rougher version of the same still life. Keep Image 1's exact objects, placement, large clear green shapes, white support bridges and orange cloth fold. Restore the distinction between an imperfect physical tool and an ideal vector path, WITHOUT restoring the chips or scattered wear from Image 2. Change only the edges and red pencil marks. Green edges should show the small, occasional blunt compression of a hand-cut card stencil: a short straight segment that meets a curve at an unequal angle, a tiny pigment bleed on one edge, a slight variation at the spout lip. The interiors remain opaque unshaded green and the entire paper remains clean white. Do not distress them. Use an actual-looking thin red coloured-pencil line whose pressure fades, breaks over the paper tooth, and briefly doubles back at the kettle rim, spout lip and pear stem. These few lines are fragile, partial observations and must not become a continuous red vector outline. Remove most of the red lower lid ring; leave only two short unequal red observations there. The contrast is a blunt reusable stencil and a searching pencil. Keep the cloth orange and the two cloth stripes intact, with an occasional direct uneven cut at the folded edge. No additional objects, drop shadows, surface modelling, speckles, fake aging or new colors. Maintain the overall simplicity.

A smaller, more useful library.

Stencil + red pencil is my first candidate for a coordinated series from this batch. It has enough contrast to hold a busy scene, and it survives without a human face. Its manufacturing logic remains stylised rather than reliable.

Carbon & citrus is useful where an image has to share space with words. It stays light, but the editor still has to decide which lines are unnecessary.

White-line engraving gives a small subject considerable weight. The still life benefits; the casual conversation becomes solemn. Its density is a choice with consequences.

The useful reusable unit is the rule about a mark, the palette and the space around it. A familiar colour system alone will not give us a distinctive hand. These examples make that limitation visible.

There is one first pass per combination. This is evidence from three scenes, not a promise of consistency in every project. Characters are not identity-locked, and the bicycle pictures are not maintenance instructions.
