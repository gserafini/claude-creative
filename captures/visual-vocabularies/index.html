01 / Drypoint

The searching line

A line searches; it does not seal every edge.

Fine scratched contours, intermittent velvety black, local hatching and large unprinted areas.

Could serve: Quiet portraits, essays, intimate chapter openers.

What I see

An attentive face and an excellent range of thin and heavy marks. It is still more fully modelled than the prompt asked. This is a familiar print vocabulary, not yet a signature.

The person is absorbed in the small task, with no smile toward the viewer. No knife. The same scene will be explored in different media; this image must commit to ONE specific material language. Style/medium: a spare drypoint impression on clean pale ivory rag paper. Everything is made from scratched black lines: fine wiry searching contours, occasional heavy velvety burr at a changed direction, small knots of irregular crosshatching. A line is allowed to stop before an outline closes. No filled flat shapes, no washes, no color; even the orange and blue bowl are represented only in black ink. Let three lightly contradictory marks remain around the cheek and wrist, as observation rather than cartoon wobble. The face should feel specifically observed, middle-aged and tired around the eyes, drawn with very few unequal strokes. The chair and lower torso are mostly omitted. About two thirds of the image remains completely unprinted; the open window is suggested by only two unjoined lines. Intimate close composition with head and working hands offset toward the lower right; the long orange peel supplies the loose visual counterline. Shading is local and sparse, not an all-over field of hatch. The needle, ink and pressure determine the edges. Avoid generalized distressed paper, sepia, perfect vector contours, symmetrical hatching, cute cartoon features, decorative border, photorealistic rendering.

The real process scratches directly into a plate; its raised burr holds ink and gives a softer, richer line. The Met · Drypoint ↗

A dark ink field, rag-wiped light, lost edges and one small warm accent.

My strongest mood study. The shoulder disappears into the room and the orange holds the attention. The face is too conventionally resolved for the broad wipes requested; this is monotype-inspired rather than a convincing technical specimen.
